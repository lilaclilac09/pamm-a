The Simulation Arena

10,000 steps. Prices drift. Predators attack. Customers arrive. Here is every frame of that battle.

Meet the four players

📈
Price Process

The "GBM" random walk. Each step, the fair price of token X drifts up or down by a random amount. This is the ground truth that other actors react to.

🦈
Arbitrageur

The predator. After every price move, the arbitrageur computes the optimal trade to extract maximum profit from any gap between your AMM price and the new fair price.

🛍️
Retail Trader

The customer. Arrives each step with a random order (buy or sell). The router compares your AMM against the normalizer and sends the order wherever the trader gets a better deal.

⚙️
Your AMM

Your formula. You receive every trade and decide how many tokens to give out. Your goal is to earn more from retail spread than you lose to arb — across every step of every market.

Each of 1,000 markets is different

The simulation runs 1,000 independent markets. Every market draws its parameters fresh from uniform distributions. Your AMM must survive all of them — calm markets, wild markets, and everything between.

VOLATILITY
0.01% — 0.70%
Sigma per step. At max vol, price can move ±2% in a single step.
RETAIL FLOW
λ 0.4 — 1.2/step
Lambda controls retail arrival rate. Order sizes: 12–28 Y units each.
NORMALIZER FEE
30 — 80 bps
Your opponent's fee varies per market. Sometimes you face a tight-spread normalizer; sometimes a greedy one.
NORMALIZER LIQUIDITY
0.4× — 2.0×
Base reserves scaled randomly. A 2× normalizer has shallower price impact per unit, making it harder to outcompete on large orders.

Arbitrageur

Both AMMs are stale at 2.00 Y/X. I ran golden-section search. Optimal trade: sell 43 X into each AMM to push prices down to fair value.

⚙️

Arb trade executed. I gave out 84.2 Y for 43 X. At fair value that 43 X is only worth 84.4 Y. Lost 0.2 Y to adverse selection this step.

🛍️

Checked both AMMs. Your AMM offers a slightly better rate for the first 12 Y. I am splitting: 12 Y goes to Your AMM, 8 Y goes to Normalizer.

⚙️

Retail trade done. Gave out 6.07 X for 12 Y. At fair price 6.07 X is worth 11.9 Y — I kept the 0.1 Y spread as profit. Net this step: −0.2 + 0.1 = −0.1 Y edge.

The engine loop, decoded

CODE 

for step in 0..config.n_steps {
  let fair = price.step();
  arb.execute_arb(&mut amm_sub, fair);
  arb.execute_arb(&mut amm_norm, fair);
  let orders = retail.generate_orders();
  for order in &orders {
    router.route_order(
      order, &mut amm_sub,
      &mut amm_norm, fair);
  }
}
PLAIN ENGLISH

Run this sequence 10,000 times (n_steps).

Advance the GBM clock: generate a new fair price for this step.

The arbitrageur attacks YOUR submission AMM first, extracting any profit from the price gap.

The same arbitrageur also attacks the normalizer, resetting it too.

A random number of retail orders appear for this step (could be 0, 1, 2, or more).

For each order, the router decides how to split it optimally between the two AMMs.
